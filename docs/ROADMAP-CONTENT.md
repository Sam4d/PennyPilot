# Penny Pilot: foundations to finance mastery

Planning draft — 18 October 2026 (India). **This document proposes a curriculum; it does not add playable lessons.** The owner approved the next-ten foundation sequence for authoring on 23 October 2026. The app now contains 20 editorial-preview lessons, 160 exercises and eight insurance guides. Authoring approval is not financial or native-language sign-off. The 16-world catalogue contains 189 units and 946 named skills; their outlines are not finished teaching content.

## The standard we are aiming for

A learner starting with no financial vocabulary should eventually be able to explain, calculate, model and critically evaluate the main decisions taught in an undergraduate finance core. That includes accounting and economics, quantitative reasoning, personal finance, corporate finance, investments, valuation, risk, financial institutions and applied analysis. Knowing trading terminology or completing multiple-choice questions is insufficient.

**Academic degree equivalence: Not verified.** This is not a university qualification, accreditation, credit transfer or a promise that an app replaces a BS in Finance. External university-by-university benchmarking, assessment moderation and faculty review are outstanding. The target is demonstrable finance competence; general-education requirements, university teaching and supervised assessment are outside the current product. We must not market a planned syllabus as an awarded degree.

A working planning allowance is 500–800 hours including reading, boards, problem sets, cumulative revision and projects. This is an **assumption dated 18 October 2026**, not available content, measured study time or a university credit conversion. Re-estimate after several full units have been authored and tested with beginners. Do not multiply 946 titles by a fixed short duration and call that educational depth.

## Curriculum architecture: four genuinely different passes

| Pass | Learner work | Evidence before advancing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Understand | Read a short passage, recall a prerequisite, predict a result, manipulate a board, explain the direction of change | Can explain the idea without repeating a memorised definition |
| 2. Calculate | Work a transparent example; solve a similar problem with different numbers and units | Correct working, units, timing and interpretation; arithmetic alone is insufficient |
| 3. Apply | Read a document, compare alternatives, state assumptions, handle an unfamiliar case | Chooses a suitable model and explains what it omits |
| 4. Evaluate | Build or audit a model, stress assumptions, challenge a claim, write a recommendation with uncertainty and sources | Independently checks a result, identifies limits and defends a reasoned decision |

These passes require separate authored content and assessment. The 18 short drafts are starter material, not 18 fully mastered four-pass skills. Give a learner a soft prerequisite suggestion, a recap and a diagnostic question; keep all content open. Placement may recommend a starting point after showing reasoning, not lock away foundations.

## A lesson is a teaching sequence, not eight disconnected questions

1. **Recall:** one prerequisite question from an earlier topic. Explain why it matters now.
2. **Purpose:** one concrete decision and a short everyday story; define unfamiliar vocabulary in brackets.
3. **Passages:** usually 60–120 words per passage, one idea per passage. Introduce a relationship, a boundary and a worked example across separate passages. Some mathematics/document lessons need more; word counts guide clarity, not padding.
4. **Prediction:** ask what should happen before showing a result.
5. **Teaching board:** manipulate a few meaningful inputs, inspect the working, compare a counterexample and reset. Explain after the attempt; keep an optional text explanation available immediately for accessibility.
6. **Independent work:** mix numeric answers, document interpretation, sequencing, classifications and explanations. Avoid distractors that only test English fluency.
7. **Connection:** explicitly link today's idea to an earlier one and a later application.
8. **Retrieval and transfer:** revisit with different numbers, circumstances or documents; explain a common wrong answer.
9. **Notebook:** save a concise principle, the worked calculation, assumptions and one practical check.
10. **Status and sources:** Draft/Reviewed(date)/Needs verification. A source URL is not proof that it was read or that a changing rule remains current.

### Passage-style proposal (not a new published lesson)

Existing concept: income versus wealth. Proposed English style: “Your salary tells you how much money arrives over a month. It does not tell you how much you own today. To answer that second question, list the current value of what you own (assets) and subtract what you still owe (liabilities). If salary is already sitting in your bank balance, do not add it a second time.”

Proposed Hindi style: “सैलरी बताती है कि एक महीने में कितने पैसे आते हैं। इससे यह नहीं पता चलता कि आज आपके पास कितना है। आज की चीज़ों की कीमत (एसेट) जोड़ें और बचा कर्ज़ (लायबिलिटी) घटाएं। सैलरी के पैसे बैंक बैलेंस में पहले ही हैं तो दोबारा न जोड़ें।”

Proposed later accounting passage: “A business can make a sale before it receives the money. Recording the sale helps measure activity over the period (revenue), while recording the cash receipt shows when money actually arrives. To judge whether the business can pay tomorrow's bill, inspect the cash flow as well as profit. This is the same stock/flow distinction you used with salary, bank balance and net worth.”

Hindi style: “कंपनी ने सामान बेच दिया, पर पैसे अगले महीने मिलेंगे। बिक्री दर्ज होना और बैंक में पैसे आना अलग बातें हैं। मुनाफ़ा देखकर अकेले यह नहीं पता चलता कि कल का बिल भर पाएंगे या नहीं। कैश फ़्लो भी देखें। यही फर्क हमने सैलरी, बैंक बैलेंस और नेट वर्थ में सीखा था।”

Proposed advanced passage style: “A valuation brings cash flows from different dates to a common date (discounting). The result depends on when cash arrives, the rate used and the assumptions behind the forecast. Change one assumption and compare the result before arguing for a single number. A precise spreadsheet output is not proof that the forecast is precise.” This returns to goal planning and compound interest without repeating a beginner question unchanged.

A later corporate-finance passage should return to this distinction: revenue, profit, cash flow and assets measure different things. The connection must be taught explicitly rather than assumed.

## Repetition that develops understanding

Use immediate retrieval, then proposed checkpoints around one day, one week, one month and three months, adapted to the learner's answers. These intervals are an initial teaching-design assumption, not a promise about memory. Existing revision scheduling is a starter implementation; cumulative mixed problems and explanatory assessment still need building.

| Recurring idea | First exposure | Return in intermediate work | Return in mastery work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A stock versus a flow | Salary, bank balance and net worth | Profit versus cash; balance sheet versus income statement | Working capital, enterprise value and cash-flow valuation |
| Units and time | Monthly/yearly interest; percentages versus rupees | Discount factors, real returns, loan cash flows | Bond yields, WACC, duration and derivative payoffs |
| Opportunity cost | One budget, two uses | Buy/rent, cash discount, asset allocation | Capital budgeting and scarce-capital choices |
| Nominal versus real | Basket prices and inflation | Purchasing power, real goals and wages | Real/nominal valuation and retirement scenarios |
| Compounding and discounting | Simple/compound interest | Annuities, NPV, funds and fees | DCF, bonds, pensions and scenario analysis |
| Risk versus uncertainty | Emergency needs and uncertain returns | Probability, diversification, insurance | Model risk, tails, stress tests and estimation error |
| Liquidity | Ready cash versus net worth | Settlement, funds, bond trading and property | Funding risk, collateral calls and business cash management |
| Leverage | EMI and debt service | Property borrowing and capital structure | Derivatives, solvency and cascading losses |
| Diversification and common exposure | Several holdings can share one risk | Covariance, sector/country/factor exposure | Portfolio construction and correlated stress |
| Costs, tax and incentives | Fees and misleading headline prices | Fund charges, spreads, agent incentives | Net returns, agency problems and governance |
| Evidence and ethics | Verify identity, claims and assumptions | Read filings and compare sources | Audit models, disclose conflicts and refuse unsupported certainty |

Each appearance must change the task. For example: identify cash flow → reconcile a bank statement → reconcile profit to cash → discount free cash flow → explain why a positive NPV project might still face a cash shortage. Repeating the same question with shuffled choices does not meet this standard.

## Sequence and priorities across the 16 worlds

The library order and stable IDs stay intact. Recommended study order can cross worlds: immediate fraud safety and money basics; essential protection and debt; quantitative/accounting/economics foundations; investing and corporate finance; then deeper asset classes, taxation/retirement, portfolio integration and optional specialist topics. Trading-heavy modules do not displace accounting, mathematics, ethics or insurance.

### World 0 — Money foundations: from zero vocabulary to a usable household plan

- Number sense: rupee grouping, fractions, percentages, percentage points, ratios, estimates and checking whether an answer is plausible. Teach calculators as checking tools, not substitutes for understanding.
- Income: employment, irregular and business income; a fictional payslip; CTC versus gross versus deductions versus take-home. Do not present CTC as cash available for monthly spending.
- Cash flow and budgeting: needs, wants and commitments; fixed versus variable costs; irregular-income buffers; known future bills versus emergencies; a budget that reconciles rather than an inflexible universal ratio.
- Banking and payments: savings/current accounts as relevant, statements, UPI/debit/credit distinctions, failed or mistaken payments, interest conventions and deposit-risk questions. Current protections and complaint routes require dated verification.
- Interest: simple/compound, rate/time units, annuities, inflation, real return and opportunity cost. Revisit these concepts before any investment product.
- Borrowing: principal/interest/fees, reducing versus flat calculations, amortisation, cash received versus amount repayable, card minimum due, credit score concepts, debt prioritisation and affordability under an income shock.
- Protection and fraud: emergency access, health/term fundamentals, exclusions and claims; phishing/OTP/SIM-swap/fake KYC, impersonation and investment traps, what evidence to preserve and how to find current official help.
- Goals and net worth: dated goals, current asset values, outstanding debt, double-counting, cash versus illiquid assets, household trade-offs and regular review.

**Boards:** a monthly cash-flow ledger with irregular income; a payslip-to-bank bridge; fee-inclusive loan timeline; a household shock board that makes known bills, emergency savings and insured losses separate.

**Recurring links:** stock/flow, units, opportunity cost, liquidity and incentives. **Assessment:** reconcile a fictional household pack, identify a scam claim, calculate a saving/debt plan and explain its limits. No real account numbers or personal financial disclosure required.

### World 1 — Economics and quantitative reasoning

- Microeconomics: scarcity, trade-offs, marginal thinking, demand/supply, elasticity, competition, externalities and information problems. Use numbers and decisions rather than memorised curve names.
- Macroeconomics: output versus growth, inflation measures and personal baskets, employment, business cycles, monetary/fiscal channels and banking/credit creation. Distinguish a relationship from a prediction.
- Institutions: central bank, banks, markets, government budgets and financial intermediaries; verify Indian institutional details before publication.
- Quantitative bridge: algebra and rearranging formulas, exponents/logarithms for growth, time lines, probability, expected values, dispersion, percentiles, sampling and uncertainty.
- Data literacy: base effects, index numbers, nominal versus real series, annualised versus total returns, misleading scales, missing observations and survivorship bias.
- Statistical inference: sample versus population, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing and practical significance. Introduce regression with residuals, confounding and correlation-versus-causation; leave advanced proofs optional.

**Boards:** a price/quantity experiment; inflation basket reweighting; a sampling simulator; two correlated data series with a hidden common cause. **Assessment:** interpret an economic claim and a data table, quantify uncertainty and identify what the data cannot establish.

### World 2 — Investment decisions before investment products

- Investment versus saving/speculation; goal horizon, liquidity, loss capacity and willingness to tolerate losses.
- Time value in full: present/future value, irregular cash flows, annuities, discount rates, nominal/real consistency and effective annual rates.
- Return measurement: income plus price movement, arithmetic versus geometric averages, money-weighted versus time-weighted performance and sequence dependence.
- Risk: probability/scenarios, volatility as an incomplete measure, drawdowns, permanent loss, inflation, credit and liquidity risks.
- Asset classes and ownership claims; diversification and common exposures; the role of benchmarks and written investment policy.
- Behaviour, incentives and protection: overconfidence, loss aversion, recency, product incentives, fees and unsupported promises. Explain why more risk does not guarantee more realised return.

**Boards:** irregular-cash-flow time line; a loss/recovery comparison; diversified holdings hit by a common shock; fee drag with explicit assumptions. **Assessment:** prepare a reasoned hypothetical goal policy and reject an unsuitable product without naming a universally “best” investment.

### World 3 — Mutual funds and pooled investing

- Pooling, units, NAV, assets and liabilities; what a unit holder owns; subscription/redemption and operational timing.
- Equity/debt/hybrid and active/passive approaches; mandates, benchmarks, concentration and credit/duration exposure.
- SIP versus lump sum as cash-flow patterns, not guaranteed-return products; what averaging can and cannot do.
- Expense ratios, transaction effects, tracking difference/error, turnover and disclosure reading; changing taxes/loads are versioned rules, not hard-coded prose.
- Read a fictional factsheet, portfolio and risk disclosure; compare like mandates and time periods, including poor periods and survivorship.
- Fund overlap, manager/style change, distribution versus accumulation concepts and suitability to a goal.

**Boards:** unit ledger around a NAV change; SIP/lump-sum scenarios including losses; overlapping holdings; gross-to-net benchmark comparison. **Assessment:** compare three fictional factsheets using costs, exposure, evidence and constraints—not a past-return ranking.

### World 4 — Markets, instruments and financial institutions

- Primary/secondary markets, equity ownership, debt claims, intermediaries, exchanges, clearing, custody and settlement. Current Indian procedures need verification.
- Order types, bids/asks, depth, spreads, slippage, liquidity and execution risk; an order is not a guaranteed fill.
- Corporate actions and share counts; dividends, splits, rights, dilution and economic value versus mechanical price changes.
- Financial statements first pass: the accounting equation, double-entry intuition, accruals, revenue/expense recognition, depreciation, inventory, receivables and payables.
- Build linked income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement; reconcile profit to cash and recognise estimates/limits.
- Banks and non-bank intermediaries: funding, maturity transformation, liquidity/credit risk and systemic links; no universal solvency rule assumed.

**Boards:** a small order book with partial fills; a split/dilution ledger; transaction-by-transaction double-entry and three-statement bridge. **Assessment:** explain a trade's execution and reconcile a small company's statements.

### World 5 — Corporate finance, accounting analysis and valuation

This world must become a core spine, not just a collection of stock ratios.

- Accounting analysis: accounting policies, footnotes, earnings quality, working capital, non-cash items, consolidation basics and limitations of ratios.
- Managerial finance: contribution, break-even, operating leverage, relevant/incremental costs, sunk costs and cash conversion. Keep cost-accounting depth tied to decisions.
- Capital budgeting: incremental after-tax cash flows, opportunity costs, NPV, IRR and its multiple-root/reinvestment pitfalls, payback limitations, mutually exclusive projects and capital constraints.
- Financing: cost of debt/equity, CAPM and estimation limits, WACC consistency, capital structure, distress, agency costs, payout and working-capital policy.
- Valuation: enterprise/equity bridges, FCFF/FCFE, terminal assumptions, economic profit, comparable multiples and comparable-company pitfalls. Distinguish a model value from an executable price.
- Business analysis: unit economics, competitive dynamics, reinvestment needs, growth versus value creation and management incentives; use evidence from filings.
- Corporate events: dilution, acquisitions, synergies, financing, control and governance. Explain incentives and uncertainty rather than predicting deal outcomes.
- Integrated modelling: linked statements, assumptions, checks, circularity, scenarios and sensitivity; a spreadsheet must balance and use consistent units/time.

**Boards:** a cash-conversion cycle; capital-budgeting board with a sunk-cost trap; WACC/DCF sensitivity surface; enterprise-to-equity bridge. **Assessment:** build and audit a fictional three-statement/DCF model, compare a project choice and write a limited, sourced valuation memo.

### World 6 — Market data, empirical analysis and disciplined risk

- Returns data, adjustments, missing observations, benchmark construction and sample selection.
- Descriptive statistics, drawdown, correlation, regression interpretation, residual risk and rolling estimates.
- Backtesting limits: transaction costs, look-ahead bias, survivorship, multiple testing, parameter tuning, out-of-sample checks and regime changes.
- Position sizing and risk budgets as scenario tools; explain that stop orders may gap and loss limits are not guarantees.
- Technical patterns are an optional critical-literacy module: describe how claims are tested and may fail; do not teach pattern recognition as dependable forecasting.

**Boards:** a backtest with a deliberate future-data leak; a cost/slippage toggle; a correlation estimate that changes across samples. **Assessment:** identify why a visually impressive historical result is not reliable evidence. This world gets less priority than accounting, economics and protection.

### World 7 — Indexing and ETFs

- Index purpose, constituent selection, weighting, rebalancing, float adjustments and concentration.
- Tracking difference/error, fees, turnover, distributions and fund/ETF implementation differences.
- ETF structure, creation/redemption intuition, NAV versus market price, premiums/discounts and execution liquidity.
- Market-cap versus alternative weighting and factor tilts: evidence, turnover, capacity, valuation and unintended bets.
- Benchmark choice, overlapping exposures, currency and country concentration.

**Boards:** build a tiny index and rebalance it; separate ETF spread from fund expenses; a stress example where indicative value and executable price differ. **Assessment:** select and critique a benchmark and compare two fictional implementations.

### World 8 — Fixed income and credit

- Bond cash-flow time lines, coupon versus yield, price/yield direction, accrued interest and clean/dirty prices.
- Present value, spot/forward/yield concepts, term structure and reinvestment assumptions; distinguish yield measures from realised return.
- Duration, convexity, rate shocks, immunisation intuition and cash-flow matching; specify compounding and shock size.
- Credit/default/recovery, seniority, covenants, ratings and their limits, spreads and liquidity.
- Government/corporate instruments, callable/convertible features at the appropriate depth; deposits and bond funds have different structures and risks.
- Fixed-income portfolio applications and maturity versus duration; current product terms require sources.

**Boards:** bond cash flows and price/yield; duration approximation versus repricing; default/recovery waterfall; an asset/liability matching exercise. **Assessment:** value a bond, explain a rate/credit shock and propose a hypothetical liability-matching plan with caveats.

### World 9 — Gold, commodities and real-return claims

- Commodity exposure versus operating-company shares; storage, carrying costs, scarcity, supply chains and currency effects.
- Spot/futures intuition, term structure, roll yield, collateral and why a rising spot price may not imply the same investment return.
- Gold formats, custody and counterparty questions; current Indian product availability/taxes must be verified.
- Portfolio role, correlation instability, liquidity and scenario comparisons after costs.

**Boards:** spot price versus rolling-futures exposure; physical carrying costs; currency-adjusted returns. **Assessment:** distinguish the asset's price change from the learner's net return and identify hidden counterparty/cost exposure.

### World 10 — Property and real-asset decisions

- Purchase/rent cash flows, transaction and maintenance costs, vacancy, financing, opportunity costs and illiquidity.
- Operating income, capitalisation rates, discounted cash flow and assumption sensitivity; avoid confusing gross rent with spendable profit.
- Loan leverage, rate resets, refinancing and stress affordability; property value and loan balance both belong in net worth.
- Title/document due diligence concepts, development/operational risks and legal verification boundaries.
- REIT structures and exposure differences; current rules and distributions require verified sources.

**Boards:** a multi-year buy/rent timeline; rental-property operating ledger; leveraged downside and vacancy stress. **Assessment:** evaluate a fictional property proposal and document why a simple rent-versus-EMI comparison is incomplete.

### World 11 — Derivatives, hedging and financial risk

- Forwards/futures/options as contracts, payoff versus profit, notional versus cash invested, collateral, margin and settlement risk.
- Hedging an actual exposure versus adding a leveraged bet; basis risk and imperfect hedges.
- Option intrinsic/time value, put/call payoffs, break-even and maximum-loss caveats; contract specifications must be verified.
- No-arbitrage/replication intuition, binomial pricing, volatility and model assumptions. Introduce Black–Scholes and Greeks only after probability/discounting prerequisites.
- Sensitivity, nonlinearity, time decay, volatility changes and tail events; demonstrate model risk and gaps.
- FX exposures, simple interest-rate/currency hedging intuition and counterparty risk connect to corporate finance.

**Boards:** payoff versus profit with fees/premium; margin under a gap; a producer/importer's hedge with basis risk; a two-step binomial tree. **Assessment:** hedge a fictional exposure, identify remaining risk and refuse an inappropriate leveraged trade. No live trading or recommendation is needed.

### World 12 — Insurance and household risk management

- Risk pooling, insurable loss and economic role; probability/severity and why affordable routine costs differ from catastrophic losses.
- Policy documents: definitions, cover, exclusions, waiting periods, deductibles, co-pay, sub-limits, disclosure and continuity. Never imply a single term applies to every policy.
- Health: eligible versus excluded costs, reimbursement/cashless processes, caps, top-ups, restoration and coordination examples; actual wording prevails.
- Life and income protection: dependency needs, pure cover versus savings-linked designs, opportunity costs and exclusions. A model result is not a personalised cover recommendation.
- Property, vehicle, travel, liability and business cover where relevant; overlap and gaps.
- Claims, evidence, complaints, settlement measures and misleading comparisons; current complaint routes/timelines require primary-source review.
- Advanced comparison: cash-flow timing, inflation, renewability, concentration of exclusions, insurer/counterparty questions and incentives.

**Boards:** claim waterfall including deductible/co-pay/cap; top-up versus super-top-up across several bills; dependent-support scenarios; a policy comparison that reveals a critical exclusion. **Assessment:** explain a fictional claim outcome and build a documented household risk map without endorsing a product.

### World 13 — Tax, retirement and long-term obligations

- Why taxation exists, tax base versus rate, marginal versus average rate, withholding versus final liability and record keeping.
- Indian salary, investment and capital-gains concepts; filing/document vocabulary and dates. Old/new-regime comparison is a versioned calculation, never timeless copy.
- Retirement: accumulation/withdrawal cash flows, inflation, longevity, sequence risk, uncertainty and liquidity; reject a universal safe withdrawal promise.
- EPF/PPF/NPS/pensions and other relevant savings structures: eligibility, access, costs, risks and taxation only after official verification.
- Estate/nomination/will and family continuity concepts; identify when qualified legal/tax advice is needed.

**Boards:** generic marginal brackets with clearly fictional numbers; verified-year withholding reconciliation when approved; retirement sequence scenarios; liquidity versus lock-in. **Assessment:** explain a hypothetical tax working using a stated year/rule version, and stress a retirement plan with uncertainty. No unverified current slab enters the app.

### World 14 — Portfolio construction, performance and governance

- Objectives and constraints; asset allocation, rebalancing, diversification, correlations and factor/currency/sector exposures.
- Expected return versus realised return; covariance, efficient-frontier intuition, CAPM/multifactor models and unstable estimates.
- Risk measures: volatility, drawdown, tracking risk, VaR/expected shortfall limits, scenario stress and liquidity. Tail risks are not eliminated by a tidy chart.
- Performance: time-/money-weighted returns, benchmark appropriateness, attribution, costs/tax and risk-adjusted metrics with caveats.
- Behavioural and institutional governance: policy, documentation, incentives, monitoring, conflicts and disciplined review.

**Boards:** portfolio exposures rather than just ticker counts; an optimiser whose recommended weights change with small input errors; rebalancing with costs; a performance attribution bridge. **Assessment:** produce a hypothetical investment policy and defend/revise a constrained portfolio under a common stress scenario.

### World 15 — Applied finance, international links and life-stage capstones

- Integrate household plans, business accounts, valuation, insurance, tax and portfolios; explain conflicting objectives and opportunity costs.
- International finance: exchange-rate quotations, currency return decomposition, parity intuition, cross-border funding/operating exposure, country risk and regulatory limits. Current laws require verification.
- Financial technology and digital safety: payments, consent, identity, operational/cyber risk, model/AI claims and evidence quality; avoid trend-driven product promotion.
- Ethics and professional communication: suitability boundaries, conflicts, data provenance, uncertainty, clear writing and respectful handling of financial distress.
- Life-stage cases: irregular income, family obligations, education, housing, self-employment, retirement and a shock/recovery plan. Do not assume one income, household structure or risk tolerance.

**Boards:** linked household decisions where one rupee cannot fund two goals; a business FX exposure; a model audit with inconsistent units, hidden fees and an unsupported growth assumption. **Assessment:** an independent capstone pack with calculations, sources, risks, alternatives and an oral/written defence.

## Interactive teaching-board contract

Every board needs a prediction, labelled controls, units, assumptions, a reset, text-equivalent results, keyboard/touch alternatives, negative/zero boundary cases and an explanation of what the model leaves out. No board may imply that a hypothetical rate is a current offer. Show working, not only an attractive final chart.

| Board prototype | Learner manipulates | Visible working and insight | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cash-flow ledger | Income dates, fixed/variable costs, known future bills | Opening cash + inflows − outflows = closing cash; timing can cause a shortage despite a positive month | Not a bank account connection |
| Three-statement sandbox | Inventory purchase, credit sale, cash collection, depreciation | Journal entries, linked statements and profit-to-cash bridge | Fictional company; no implied accounting-standard certification |
| Time-value timeline | Rate, timing, contribution, withdrawal | Discount factor and each cash flow's present value | State rate/period convention and fees |
| Borrowing board | Term, fee, rate, cash received | Payment split and full cash-flow cost | Actual quotes/variable rates may differ |
| Risk/common-shock board | Holding weights and shared exposures | Individual outcomes versus combined exposure | Correlation and distribution are assumptions |
| Claim waterfall | Eligible bill, excluded bill, deductible, co-pay, remaining cover | Each rupee allocated once; amount paid by each party reconciles | Actual policy ordering/wording takes precedence |
| Corporate project board | Investment, incremental cash flows, discount rate | NPV/IRR plus a sunk-cost/opportunity-cost counterexample | Forecast uncertainty and capital constraints remain |
| Valuation board | Revenue drivers, margins, reinvestment, discount/terminal assumptions | Linked statements and enterprise/equity bridge | A model range is not a target-price promise |
| Bond board | Coupon, yield, maturity, shock, recovery | Cash-flow PV and duration approximation error | Conventions, credit and liquidity matter |
| Derivative hedge board | Underlying exposure, hedge size, price path, basis | Payoff, profit, cash collateral and residual exposure | Margin calls/contract terms can dominate final payoff |
| Retirement board | Contributions, real spending, sequence, longevity | Cash balances and failure scenarios rather than a single smooth path | No universal withdrawal rate or personal advice |
| Evidence audit board | Reveal footnotes, fees, data leaks, conflicts | A conclusion changes as hidden assumptions are corrected | Visual appeal does not establish causation |

These are **mock-board design proposals**, not 12 implemented new simulators. Existing lesson boards are the engineering starting point. Require independent arithmetic tests, conservation/reconciliation checks and useful failure cases before authoring advanced boards.

## Assessment and mastery evidence

Proposed mastery thresholds are teaching-design assumptions, not earned certificates: aim for at least 80% on mixed retrieval plus a correct explanation of all safety-critical decisions; allow calm retries. More important than a threshold is evidence on unseen cases. A high average must not excuse approving a fraud prompt, ignoring leverage loss or presenting an unverified rule as current.

1. **Foundation checkpoint:** reconcile a household ledger, read a payslip/bank statement, separate planned/emergency costs, explain debt and identify fraud.
2. **Quantitative/accounting checkpoint:** convert units, discount irregular cash flows, explain sampling uncertainty and link three statements.
3. **Corporate-finance project:** compare incremental projects, estimate a documented cost of capital, build/check a DCF and explain sensitivity.
4. **Investments/fixed-income project:** compare fictional fund/bond disclosures, calculate net returns and apply a common risk scenario.
5. **Protection/tax/retirement case:** interpret a fictional claim, use an explicitly verified tax year when available, and stress long-term spending.
6. **Independent capstone:** a household or small-business investment-policy/valuation pack with an auditable model, reference log, limitations and a plain-language defence.

Financial and language reviewers must moderate advanced work. Automated quizzes cannot certify valuation judgement, ethical reasoning or degree equivalence. Add self-explanation and model-audit evidence before awarding any future mastery badge; no app certificate should imply professional authorisation.
